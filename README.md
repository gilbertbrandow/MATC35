# MATC35 - Number Theory

MATC35 Number Theory, 7.5 credits, is an elective first-cycle course for a Bachelor of Science degree in mathematics. It may also be taken as a stand-alone course. The course is taught in English at 50% study pace during the second half of the autumn semester, from 2 November 2026 to 17 January 2027.

The course develops classical number theory and its applications, with particular emphasis on mathematical reasoning, proof, problem solving, and connections to cryptography.

## Course content

The course covers:

- Multiplicative number-theoretic functions and the Möbius inversion formula
- Euler's phi-function and Euler's generalization of Fermat's theorem
- Primitive roots and indices
- Quadratic residues and Euler's criterion
- The Legendre symbol and quadratic reciprocity
- Representations of integers as sums of squares
- Number-theoretic properties of the Fibonacci sequence
- Continued fractions and Diophantine approximation
- Applications to cryptography

## Learning outcomes

After completing the course, the student should be able to:

- Give a detailed account of the concepts and methods included in the course
- Identify the most important theorems and present their proofs
- Explain the theory behind the methods introduced in the course
- Combine knowledge from different parts of the course when solving problems
- Identify problems suited to number-theoretic methods and apply appropriate techniques
- Plan and complete relevant assignments within a given time frame
- Explain mathematical solutions coherently in speech and writing
- Discuss the importance and limitations of number theory in areas such as encryption, including relevant social and ethical aspects

## Assessment

Passing the course requires passing all three components:

- A compulsory written assignment, implemented in 2026 as a weekly course journal
- A written examination
- An oral examination

The oral examination may only be taken after passing the written examination.

The ordinary written examination takes place on Thursday, 17 December 2026, 08:00-13:00 in MA 9. The resit written examination takes place on Tuesday, 12 January 2027, 08:00-13:00 in MH:309A-C and MH:332A-B. Oral-examination dates have not yet been published.

The grading scale is Fail, Pass, and Pass with distinction. Each individual examination component is graded Fail or Pass. Pass with distinction requires at least 75% of the combined maximum points from the written and oral examinations; their maximum points are weighted 3:1.

## Course journal

A handwritten journal entry of about one A4 page and at least approximately 300 words is required each week. Digital handwriting is accepted, but the entry must be submitted on paper, either during a lecture or at the lecturer's office.

All but one of the assigned entries must be submitted to pass the course. Each entry is due before the second lecture, on Friday, in the following week.

Each entry contains:

- A plain-language explanation of the published weekly topic
- A brief evaluation of the week's lecture material, seminar exercises, study method, and learning outcome
- A "Pick of the Week" reflecting on any course-related idea, theorem, exercise, application, or experience

The preliminary overview still labels cryptography as a written assignment. The current Math Diary page explicitly states that the previous extensive written assignment has been replaced by the weekly course journal, so no separate cryptography submission is assumed unless the course information is updated.
